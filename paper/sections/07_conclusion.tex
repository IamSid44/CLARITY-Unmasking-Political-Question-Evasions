\section{Conclusion and Plan to the Final Evaluation}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A carefully controlled encoder for CLARITY stops at 0.384 Subtask~2 macro-F1 per model (0.405 as a system), and the evidence places the limit in the model rather than in the labels or the decision rule.
Changing only the backbone to a LoRA-tuned 8B decoder improves both subtasks on all ten paired seeds and lifts the system to 0.543 / 0.746, still below the best multi-call pipeline (0.617) and the human reference (0.684).

\paragraph{Plan.}
The remaining work (Table~\ref{tab:timeline}; none of it run yet) asks two questions that the stronger backbone makes worth asking.
\emph{Does structure help once knowledge is no longer the bottleneck?}
The hierarchy designs of \S\ref{sec:encoder} failed on an encoder whose errors came from missing knowledge, while the organisers found hierarchical use of the taxonomy most effective for LLM systems \citep{thomas-etal-2026-semeval}.
Qwen's remaining errors now sit mostly inside the Ambivalent branch (\S\ref{sec:llm}), exactly where branch specialists and boundary experts act, so we re-test each design on the Qwen system, one change at a time and with predictions registered first.
\emph{How much accuracy does each unit of cost buy?}
An acceptable label is in Qwen's top three for 93.1\% of items, and its confidence tracks correctness, so a cascade can send only the least-confident items, with Qwen's top candidates, to a larger open LLM given label definitions and boundary examples.
Sweeping the deferred fraction from 0 to 100\% gives macro-F1 against LLM calls, tokens and GPU cost per item, on the same axes as TeleAI's 1.94 calls per item.

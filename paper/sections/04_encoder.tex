\section{The Encoder Track}
\label{sec:encoder}

Our first system is a fine-tuned encoder. We built it one controlled change at a
time against a frozen baseline, and we judged each change by comparisons paired by
seed, under the protocol of \S\ref{sec:task}.

\paragraph{Model and training.}
We fine-tuned all 435M parameters of DeBERTa-v3-large \citep{he2023debertav3} as a
9-way classifier with \citet{wolf-etal-2020-transformers}. The \texttt{[CLS]} state
passes through a dense tanh pooler and a new linear layer over the nine evasion
labels, and Subtask~1 is read off the predicted label. The baseline input pairs the
sub-question (segment~A, at most 96 tokens) with the answer (segment~B), cut from the
right at 512 tokens. The final input puts ``Sub-question: \ldots{} Full question:
\ldots'' in segment~A, with up to 256 tokens for the journalist's whole turn, and
raises the context to 1,024 tokens. The sub-question comes first, so truncation never
removes it. We added the full question because 69\% of training rows share their
answer with another sub-question, and a model that cannot see the other
sub-questions cannot tell which part of the answer responds to which. Training used
plain cross-entropy, AdamW \citep{loshchilov2019decoupled} at a learning rate of
$10^{-5}$ with layer-wise decay 0.95 ($10^{-4}$ for the head), a cosine schedule with
10\% warm-up, batch size 16, and bf16 autocast over fp32 weights
(Appendix~\ref{sec:app-encoder}). The epoch was chosen on the train slice. The three
main configurations used seeds 0--9; a seed fixes the head initialisation and data order,
so seeds are paired across configurations.

\paragraph{What improved the model.}
Two changes improved single models (Table~\ref{tab:enc-main},
Figure~\ref{fig:enc-seeds}). Training for 16 epochs instead of 8 raised \Stwo{} by
+0.047 ($t=3.4$, 9/10 seeds up) and \Sone{} by +0.026 ($t=2.7$, 8/10). Adding the
full question at 16 epochs gave a further +0.022 \Stwo{} ($t=2.0$, 7/10) and +0.012
\Sone{} ($t=1.3$, 6/10). Together the two changes add +0.069 \Stwo{} ($t=3.6$) and
+0.038 \Sone{} ($t=2.8$), each on 9 of 10 seeds. Most of the gain comes from longer
training, which also lifted the classes the baseline handled worst: General rose from
0.119 to 0.295 per model (seeds 0--4).

Undertraining at first looked like a negative result. Our first test added the
full question together with Balanced Softmax and focal loss
\citep{ren2020balanced,lin2017focal} at 8 epochs, lost to the baseline, and was
blamed on the loss. A one-change control with plain cross-entropy at 8 epochs
scored lower still: \pmsd{0.282}{0.043} against \pmsd{0.337}{0.044} on seeds 0--4.
Its final training loss (1.32--1.50, against 0.57--1.09) showed that it had not
finished fitting; its last epoch sat where the baseline had been at epoch~3. Its
seeds were less confident (mean top probability 0.445 against 0.594) and leaned on
the class prior. After logit adjustment, single models matched the baseline (0.353
against 0.357). At 16 epochs the same input gave the best single model. The deficit
came from the training budget, not from the input or the loss, so every run now
reports its final training loss and selected epoch next to its scores.

\paragraph{From model to system.}
A system averages the ten seeds' probabilities and applies logit adjustment
(Eq.~\ref{eq:la}). On \Stwo, better single models did not give a better system.
Ensembling helped the final model more (0.384 to 0.409) than the baseline (0.315 to
0.319). Logit adjustment then lifted the baseline ensemble by +0.109 (to 0.428) and
the final ensemble not at all (0.409 to 0.405; $\tau=0.4$ when fitted on all of dev).
The final system scores 0.405 \Stwo{} and 0.648 \Sone, against 0.428 and 0.601 for
the baseline system. The \Stwo{} difference is $-$0.022, with a 95\% bootstrap
interval of [$-$0.101, +0.061] over dev items (seeds fixed), so the two systems
cannot be told apart on \Stwo; on \Sone{} the final system is ahead. Our reading
(inferred, not tested directly) is that longer training and logit adjustment
correct the same fault, the pull of a prior-leaning model towards frequent classes.
Either one is enough, and together they add nothing. The final system was chosen by
a rule written before the replication runs.

Five-seed systems are unreliable on 308 items. The baseline system scored 0.438 on
seeds 0--4 and 0.356 on seeds 5--9; the final system scored 0.478 and 0.434. This
spread between two draws of the same system is larger than most effects we wanted
to measure. All system claims therefore use ten seeds, and the conclusions rest on
per-seed paired comparisons.

One later variant helped. Training on all of train for 16 epochs and keeping the
last epoch is supported by the fact that, on the ten full-question seeds, the last
epoch already scored at least as well as the selected one (\Stwo{}
+0.011\,$\pm$\,0.020, 7/10). Paired with the full-question model on seeds 0--4, it
gained +0.029 \Stwo{} per model (4/5 seeds up) and lost 0.017 \Sone{} (1/5); its
5-seed system scored 0.489. Given the spread above, this is a 5-seed result and not
a ranking; a 10-seed run is proposed.

\paragraph{What did not help.}
Seven further ideas, each tested against a named control, left the model or the
system where it was (details in Appendix~\ref{sec:app-encoder},
Table~\ref{tab:app-negative}). A second encoder that re-ranked the baseline's top-3
labels from their definitions scored 0.354, against 0.365 for the baseline ensemble
it re-ranked. Learning what each label means from 3,448 examples is harder than
classifying them. Balanced Softmax with focal loss over-corrected towards rare
classes (Explicit F1 fell from 0.679 to 0.319) and used up the gain that logit
adjustment would have given. Three hierarchy designs did not beat the flat model:
post-hoc routing through the clarity level, a factorised output head, and a
Non-Reply gate with branch specialists. The flat softmax already scores all nine
labels jointly. Boundary experts for the three most-confused pairs changed
\Stwo{} by +0.002, because they seldom overturn a decision the flat model makes from
the same input. A uniform model soup \citep{wortsman2022soups} of the ten models
scored 0.267. Our seeds differ in the random head initialisation and the data order,
so their weights do not average. Nine per-class decision weights overfit 308
items, and a temperature with nine biases, fitted on one half of dev and scored on
the other, swung between 0.29 and 0.46. An ensemble that mixed both inputs scored 0.403, against 0.405. In
short, adding encoders or rules trained on the same 3,448 examples did not move the
system. Section~\ref{sec:llm} tests the next candidate, the backbone itself.

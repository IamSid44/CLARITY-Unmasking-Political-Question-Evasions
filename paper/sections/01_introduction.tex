\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Politicians often answer the question they would have liked to be asked.
Whether an answer gives the requested information, gives it only implicitly, or avoids it by dodging, deflecting or declining is a long-standing object of study in political communication \citep{bull1994identifying}, and \citet{thomas-etal-2024-never} turned it into a classification task with a two-level taxonomy of response clarity and evasion.
SemEval-2026 Task~6 \citep{thomas-etal-2026-semeval} made this a shared task: Subtask~2 asks for one of nine evasion types, and Subtask~1 for one of three clarity levels that the nine types determine.

The competition was won by multi-call large language model (LLM) pipelines \citep{shi-etal-2026-teleai,peerachaidecho-rutherford-2026-chulanlp}, while the organisers report that fine-tuned encoders stopped at about 0.50 macro-F1 on Subtask~2 regardless of scale or ensembling \citep{thomas-etal-2026-semeval}.
A leaderboard does not say \emph{why} encoders stop there.
The candidate explanations make different predictions: the labels may be too noisy to learn (then annotators would disagree where models fail), the metric may reward something the model is not optimising (then a better decision rule would help), the model may lack the knowledge needed to read an answer against its question (then a larger pretrained model would help under the same protocol), or the label meanings may not be learnable from 3,448 examples (then supplying definitions would help).

We study these explanations with a deliberately controlled single-pass classifier.
Every change is made one at a time against a named control, evaluated on ten paired seeds, with hypotheses and predictions written down before each run and no choice made on the development set (\S\ref{sec:task}).
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item A carefully built DeBERTa-v3-large system (\S\ref{sec:encoder}) whose two real improvements (training length and the full journalist question) are separated from a series of documented negative results on the decision layer: re-ranking, hierarchical models, rebalancing losses, per-class thresholds and model soups.
  \item Evidence that the remaining gap lies in the model rather than in label noise or the decision rule (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).
  \item A one-change test of the knowledge explanation (\S\ref{sec:llm}): replacing the backbone with Qwen3-8B-Base fine-tuned with LoRA \citep{qwen3,hu2022lora} improves both subtasks on all ten seeds, and as a ten-seed system raises Subtask~2 from 0.405 to 0.543 and Subtask~1 from 0.648 to 0.746 on the development set.
  \item An analysis of where that gain falls, which refutes our own pre-registered prediction that it would concentrate on the boundaries between commitment levels (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).
\end{itemize}
The test labels were never released and the evaluation server has closed, so all comparisons are on the 308-item development set; we report seed spreads, paired differences and bootstrap intervals throughout to state each claim at the resolution this set supports.

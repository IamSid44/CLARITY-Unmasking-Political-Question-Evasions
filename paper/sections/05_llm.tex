\section{Is the Gap Knowledge? An 8B LLM Classifier}\label{sec:llm}

The encoder track (\S\ref{sec:encoder}) and the error analysis (\S\ref{sec:analysis}) show that the model, not label noise, is what fails: many errors fall on items all three annotators agreed on, and a held-out annotator scores far above the model.
Two explanations remain.
The encoder may lack \emph{knowledge}, the world and language knowledge a 0.4B-parameter encoder has not acquired, or it may lack \emph{label meaning}, what each evasion type means, which a classifier must infer from about 3,400 examples.
A larger pretrained classifier tests the first explanation with nothing else changed.
Before any run we registered three predictions in the experiment log: the gain passes the screening bar (+0.015 on \Stwo{} with at least 2 of 3 seeds up); if knowledge is the gap, the single-model \Stwo{} mean lies in 0.43--0.50 and \Sone{} rises by +0.02 to +0.05; and the gain falls mostly on the commitment boundary (Explicit $\leftrightarrow$ Implicit, General $\leftrightarrow$ Implicit/Explicit) and more on agreed than on contested items.

\paragraph{One change: the backbone.}
We took the best encoder configuration (sub-question, full question and answer, 16 epochs) and replaced DeBERTa-v3-large \citep{he2023debertav3} with Qwen3-8B-Base \citep{qwen3}, adapted with LoRA \citep{hu2022lora} of rank 16 ($\alpha=32$, dropout 0.05) on all linear projections of the attention and feed-forward blocks.
A new 9-way linear head, trained in full, reads the hidden state of the last token.
The input text and budgets are those of the encoder: ``Sub-question: \dots{} Full question: \dots'' capped at 256 tokens, then the answer, right-truncated to fit 1,024 tokens in total.
One fixed closing question (``How does the answer respond to the sub-question?'') is appended, so the token the head reads sits at the same position in every item, after the whole question and answer.
Sequences are right-padded, and each run first checks that an item's logits are the same alone and inside a padded batch, aborting otherwise; this guards against a head that reads a padding token.
Training ran in bf16 with AdamW at a learning rate of $10^{-4}$, a cosine schedule with 5\% warmup, 3 epochs and an effective batch of 16 (micro-batch 4 $\times$ accumulation 4).
The learning rate and epoch count change because the backbone does.
Everything else is identical: the same 3,103 training rows, the same 345-row train slice (asserted identical in code), plain cross-entropy, and the epoch with the best slice macro-F1; dev is never used for a choice.
Seeds 0--9 are paired with the DeBERTa runs of the same seeds.
Appendix~\ref{sec:app-llm} lists all hyperparameters and the compute.

\paragraph{Results at ten paired seeds.}
The 3-seed screen passed the bar (+0.081 on \Stwo{}, 3 of 3 seeds up), so the run was extended to ten seeds; its mean of \pmsd{0.462}{0.080} is superseded by the ten-seed numbers in Table~\ref{tab:llm-main}.
Per model, Qwen improves \Stwo{} by +0.092 ($t=7.12$) and \Sone{} by +0.095 ($t=8.48$) on 10 of 10 seeds, more than the whole encoder track gained from its baseline (0.315) to its best model (0.384).
As a system it reaches 0.543 / 0.746 against 0.405 / 0.648 (\Stwo{} difference +0.136, 95\% bootstrap interval [+0.054, +0.224]), and the ranking holds on the 2-annotator reference sets (0.524 against 0.382).
Logit adjustment \citep{menon2021longtail} matters far more for Qwen (+0.062 per model, against +0.005 for DeBERTa); \S\ref{sec:analysis} traces this to a skewed class prior.

All registered size predictions held: \Stwo{} lies in the predicted 0.43--0.50 (the \Sone{} gain exceeded its range), the ten-seed mean stayed within 0.42--0.48, Qwen beat DeBERTa on 10 of 10 seeds (at least 7 predicted), and the system gain exceeded +0.03.
The prediction about \emph{where} the gain falls was refuted (\S\ref{sec:analysis}).
Among published dev results (Figure~\ref{fig:llm}), the single-pass system lies above TeleAI's fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B (0.495; \citealp{shi-etal-2026-teleai}) and ChulaNLP's encoder+LLM pipeline (0.52; \citealp{peerachaidecho-rutherford-2026-chulanlp}) and below TeleAI's multi-call pipeline (0.617) and the human reference (0.684); these come from other protocols, so the comparison locates our system rather than ranking it.

\paragraph{Two single-change follow-ups.}
In the 3-epoch runs the slice F1 rose at every epoch and the last epoch was always selected.
Under a cosine schedule this proves nothing, because the schedule itself favours the last epoch, where the learning rate has decayed; only a longer schedule tests undertraining.
With 12 epochs (seeds 0--2, schedule stretched accordingly) the slice selected epochs 9, 11 and 8, and the slice F1 rose by +0.084 on 3 of 3 seeds, so the rule registered in advance adopted the longer schedule.
Training loss fell to about 0.02 without the slice F1 declining.
Dev \Stwo{} was \pmsd{0.543}{0.015} (\Sone{} \pmsd{0.742}{0.008}), reported only; a ten-seed 12-epoch system is proposed, not yet run.
Training on all 3,448 rows and keeping the last of 3 epochs gave +0.017\,$\pm$\,0.045 per model (6 of 10 seeds up) and a system of 0.575, but the system difference of +0.028 has a 95\% interval of [$-$0.026, +0.092] and \Sone{} fell from 0.746 to 0.711.
As with the encoder (\S\ref{sec:encoder}), the 3-seed screen (+0.039) overstated a gain that ten seeds cannot distinguish from zero.

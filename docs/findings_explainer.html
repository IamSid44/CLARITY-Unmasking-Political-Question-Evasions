CLARITY project · findings explained step by step · 30 Oct 2026

Is evasion in the question or in the answerer?

We started with a shared task (classify how a US president's answer responds to a journalist's question) and ended with a research question: when an answer evades, is that caused by the question or by the person answering? This page walks through every finding in the order the argument needs them.

Step 0

The story in five sentences

  1. Knowledge beats architecture. A big pretrained model, fine-tuned simply, beats every clever structure we put on a smaller one; and it ranks the right label better than it picks it, so a larger model adjudicates the uncertain cases. That system is our instrument.
  2. Evasion is a relation, not a property of the answer. The same answer gets different labels for different sub-questions, far more often than annotator noise explains, and "is the answer about the question?" does not capture it.
  3. We test causally whether the instrument reads the question, by swapping and removing questions while keeping the answer fixed.
  4. We ask the central question: we give the presidents' exact questions to language models that have no political stake, and check whether they evade on the same questions.
  5. We measure cost: how early in an answer evasion becomes visible, and whether a frozen model already encodes it.
Step 1

Why we changed direction

The mid-evaluation feedback said, in short: the experiments were disjoint, several gave the expected result, parameters were changed haphazardly, and limitations were listed that could not be acted on. A literature search (seven research agents, plus one acting as a hostile reviewer) added a second problem:

What we builtWho already did it at SemEval-2026 Task 6
Small model proposes top labels, an LLM choosesChulaNLP (2nd on Subtask 2)
Confidence routing to a bigger modelTeleAI (1st), AsymVerify
Distilling a large model into a small oneYNU-HPCC

So our pipeline cannot be the contribution. It becomes the measurement instrument, and the contribution moves to what nobody in the 30 system papers did: test whether systems read the question, and use the taxonomy to measure something other than presidents. The new rules we follow from the feedback:

  • One question, one chain: every experiment answers a question raised by the previous one.
  • Predictions written down before the run, with a reason the outcome is not obvious (experiment log, E25 registration, 14:30 IST).
  • One frozen instrument: every new label comes from the same system with every parameter fixed in advance. Nothing is re-tuned on new data.
  • Every limitation paired with a test that addresses it.
Step 2

Act 1: building the instrument

All numbers are Subtask-2 macro-F1 on the 308-item dev set, where a prediction counts as correct if it matches any of the three annotators.

DeBERTa-v3-large, 10 seeds0.405
Qwen3-8B + LoRA, 10 seeds (M1)0.598
M1 + 27B adjudicator (accuracy point)0.650
Distilled student + 27B (cost point)0.608
One human vs the other two0.684

How each step forced the next

  1. Structure did not help. On DeBERTa, hierarchy, rebalanced losses, specialist experts, re-rankers and model soups all failed against paired controls. Only longer training and giving the full question helped (0.315 → 0.384 per model).
  2. So we tested knowledge. Changing only the backbone to Qwen3-8B gained more than all encoder changes together: +0.092 per model, on 10 of 10 seeds.
  3. The 8B ranks better than it decides. An acceptable label is first for 60% of items but in its top 3 for 93%. So a 27B model picks among the 8B's top 4, for only the 30% least confident items (0.36 LLM calls per item).
  4. For cost, one student trained on the 27B's soft labels replaces the 10 seeds: 0.608 at 0.27× the GPU cost.
Honest uncertainty
The 27B's gain over M1 is +0.067 with the fusion settings held fixed, but +0.037 [−0.044, +0.120] when they are refitted inside each bootstrap. On 308 items, small gains are within noise. That is one reason the paper's weight moves to findings that do not depend on a few points of F1.
Step 3

Act 2: evasion is a relation, not a property of the answer

A president's single answer often serves several sub-questions split out of one journalist turn. We call these siblings. They give a free natural experiment: same answer, different question.

Finding 1 · the data is relational
69% of training rows share their answer with a sibling. Of the 3,079 sibling pairs, 61.5% carry different evasion labels (40.6% different clarity levels).
Finding 2 · it is not annotator noise

Maybe sibling labels differ just because annotators are noisy. We tested that on dev, where each item has three annotators: how often do two labels by different annotators disagree?

Same item43%
Sibling items (same answer)72%
Random items83%
Siblings disagree far more than annotators do on one item: the label moves because the question moves.
Finding 3 · our model does read the question (we expected the opposite)
On the 58 dev sibling pairs whose annotators gave completely different labels, M1 repeats the same label only 19% of the time. But both siblings are right in only 40%: question grounding exists, and it is the bottleneck.
Finding 4 · relevance is not answerhood
Political science measures answer quality by whether the answer can be picked out from its own question (Alvarez & Morrier, 2025). We computed exactly that with the 8B model. It is ordered across labels (p = 2e-12) but barely separates replies from evasions: AUC 0.566 for Clear Reply vs the rest. Deflections (0.80) and partial answers (0.84) look as tied to their question as explicit answers (0.82). An answer can be about the question without answering it. That is exactly the gap our instrument fills.

Step 5

Act 3: does the instrument read the question? Yes, causally.

Before using the instrument to measure anything, we checked it responds to the question, not just the answer's style. We kept every answer and changed only what it was paired with. All settings were frozen beforehand.

Own question25.3%
Someone else's question1.3%
No question at all64.0%
Prediction held (H25a-1)
Paired with an unrelated question, an answer stops being a reply: 62 of 78 Clear Replies become "Dodging", exactly what the taxonomy defines. With no question at all, most answers are called Explicit (there is nothing to evade). The test set shows the same direction (33.8% → 11.0%). The instrument reads the relation, not the style.
Step 6

Act 4: question or speaker? Neither alone.

We put the presidents' exact questions (308 sub-questions) to two open language models, which have no political stake. Each condition changes one thing:

ConditionWhat changesMistral-7B evadesQwen3-8B evades
N neutral analystno stake59%51%
P as the Presidentthe role52%52%
F after "you haven't answered"pursuit23%17%
B with his own answer as notesinformation61%48%
The Presidents themselves (annotators)74%
Prediction failed (H25b-1): the question alone does not drive it
A neutral analyst does not evade more on the questions presidents evaded (odds ratio 1.18, 95% CI 0.80–1.73). If evasion were purely about hard questions, it would.
Prediction failed (H25b-2): the role does not raise the rate
Playing the President changes the overall evasion rate by −3 points (CI −8 to +2).
The interesting pattern (exploratory, needs confirming)
In the President's role, the same models evade on the same questions presidents evaded: odds ratio 2.74 (CI 1.87–4.17), in both models (Mistral 3.0, Qwen 2.5). So the role changes where models evade, not how often. It is not an instrument artefact: neutral and President answers are labelled by the same instrument on the same questions; only the answers differ.

Reading: evasion behaves like question × role. A question becomes evasion-prone when it is put to someone who holds the President's accountability.

Prediction held (H25b-3): pressing works
When the journalist says "with respect, you haven't answered", 66% of evasive answers become clear replies (only 5% go the other way).
Narrow miss (H25b-4): what the taxonomy means
Given the President's own answer as notes, models answer more clearly when that answer was indirect (Implicit, General, Partial) than when it was a non-answer (Dodging, Deflection, Declining). The difference is +11 points against a +15 bar (CI −1 to +23). Without notes it is −2, so the direction is as predicted, but not established.

Style: language models overuse Explicit (41–83% vs presidents' 28%) and rarely Dodge (2–9% vs 18%).

Step 7

Act 5: how early, how cheap?

Prediction failed (H25c): you need most of the answer
Reading half the answer gives the same label only 66% of the time, and the score drops by 0.15. Outright refusals show early ("I won't comment": 82% agreement after a quarter), but General and Dodging only show at the end. Live detection after half an answer is not viable.
Prediction failed (H25d-1): no frozen fast path
A simple probe on the untuned 8B model reaches 0.397 at its best layer, against 0.525 for one fine-tuned seed.
Prediction held (H25d-2): fine-tuning binds label to question
The frozen probe gives two sub-questions of the same answer the same label 45% of the time, against 19% for the fine-tuned model. Reading the relation is what fine-tuning teaches. Latency of the base model (batch 1, H100): 48 ms at full depth, 28 ms at half depth.
Step 8

How it all connects

Mid-eval question: what limits a classifier here? → structure doesn't help, knowledge does (one change, 10/10 seeds).
↓
The 8B ranks better than it decides (top-3 93%) → a 27B chooses among its top 4, only when unsure → distil for cost → stress-test → the instrument (0.650).
↓
The instrument isn't novel (other teams built it) → what can it measure?
↓
Evasion is a relation: the label moves with the question (72% vs 43%); relevance doesn't capture it (AUC 0.57).
↓
Does the instrument read the relation? Yes, causally (25% → 1% Clear Reply with an unrelated question).
↓
Question or speaker? Neither alone: no stake, no link; the President's role → the same questions evaded, at the same rate. Pressing works.
↓
Cost: it needs the whole answer and a fine-tuned model; fine-tuning is what binds label to question.
The thesis
Evasion is a relation between a question and an answer, and which questions get evaded depends on the role of the person answering.

Step 10

Glossary

Subtask 2 (S2) / Subtask 1 (S1)
S2: 9 evasion types (Explicit, Implicit, Dodging, General, Deflection, Partial, Declining, Claims ignorance, Clarification). S1: 3 clarity levels (Clear Reply, Ambivalent, Clear Non-Reply), fixed by the S2 type.
Any-annotator macro-F1
The official metric: a prediction counts if any annotator gave that label; F1 is averaged over the 9 classes, so rare classes matter as much as common ones.
Siblings
Sub-questions split out of one journalist turn and answered by the same answer.
M1 / M3 / instrument
M1: Qwen3-8B fine-tuned (10 seeds). M3: the 27B model that picks among M1's top-4 labels for the 30% least confident items. The instrument: both together, with every setting frozen.
Evades
In the audit: the predicted clarity level is not Clear Reply.
Odds ratio (OR)
How much more likely an LLM is to evade on questions the president evaded than on questions he answered. OR = 1 means no link.
Pre-registration
Predictions and decision rules written in the experiment log before the run, so a result cannot be reinterpreted afterwards.
